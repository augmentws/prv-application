Case Background
This matter concerns alleged failures by Florida state actors and Citizens Property Insurance Corporation during the Florida property insurance crisis associated with the 2004 and 2005 hurricane seasons. Plaintiffs are a certified class of Florida residential property owners who held homeowners insurance policies, or were denied or lost coverage, in Florida between January 1, 2004 and December 31, 2006. The class includes homeowners who experienced policy cancellations, non-renewals, or premium increases exceeding 50% during the class period, as well as homeowners insured through Citizens Property Insurance Corporation.

The defendants include the State of Florida, Office of the Governor; Citizens Property Insurance Corporation; and the Office of Insurance Regulation. The Governor's Office is alleged to have overseen and directed the state's response to the property insurance crisis, including task forces, legislative priorities, and emergency measures. Citizens was the state-created insurer of last resort and allegedly became financially strained, operationally mismanaged, and subject to governance conflicts. The Office of Insurance Regulation, or OIR, was responsible for approving or disapproving insurance rate filings, overseeing insurer solvency, and enforcing market conduct standards.

Between 2004 and 2006, Hurricanes Charley, Frances, Ivan, Jeanne, and Wilma caused substantial insured property losses in Florida. Plaintiffs allege that the Florida property insurance market deteriorated as private insurers exited the market, policies were canceled or non-renewed, premiums increased sharply, and Citizens absorbed large numbers of displaced policyholders. Plaintiffs further allege that the Governor's Office, Citizens, and OIR failed to take timely and adequate action to prevent market collapse, protect consumers from excessive rate increases, ensure Citizens' solvency, and implement known mitigation and reform measures.

Defendants deny liability and contend that the 2004–2005 hurricane losses were unprecedented, that OIR complied with its statutory duties, that Citizens operated within its legislative charter, that premium increases were actuarially justified, and that the Governor's Office pursued reasonable reform measures. Defendants also contend that many harms were caused by forces beyond their control, including global reinsurance conditions, federal flood insurance limitations, and private insurer business decisions.

Key terms include:

“Citizens” means Citizens Property Insurance Corporation, the state-created insurer of last resort.
“OIR” means the Office of Insurance Regulation, the Florida regulatory body overseeing insurance rates and market conduct.
“DFS” means the Department of Financial Services.
“FHCF” means the Florida Hurricane Catastrophe Fund, a state trust fund providing reinsurance to Florida insurers.
“Non-renewal” means an insurer's decision not to renew an existing policy at the end of its term.
“Depopulation” means transferring policies from Citizens to private insurers.
“Mitigation” and “hardening” mean physical improvements to structures, such as roof reinforcement or window protection, intended to reduce hurricane damage.
“Assessment” means a surcharge levied against Florida policyholders to cover Citizens' financial deficits.
“Reinsurance” means insurance purchased by insurance companies to limit catastrophic-loss exposure.
“Cat bond” or “catastrophe bond” means a financial instrument that transfers catastrophic risk to capital-market investors.
“JUA” means Joint Underwriting Association, a mechanism for providing insurance where coverage is otherwise unavailable.
“CHHA” means Coastal High Hazard Area, a geographic designation affecting development and insurance requirements.


Issues
Issue 1: Market Disruption and Consumer Harm
A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to the nature or extent of disruption to the Florida property insurance market during the 2004–2006 period. Responsive subject matter includes premium increases, policy cancellations, non-renewals, coverage restrictions, insurer exits, unavailability of coverage, and other effects on Florida property owners.

A document is also responsive to this issue if it relates to the impact of the insurance crisis on Florida homeowners, businesses, or communities, including effects on property transactions, economic development, housing affordability, financial hardship, or community-level disruption.

A document is responsive to this issue if it reflects information received by the Governor's Office from constituents, state agencies, legislators, industry participants, or other sources concerning the scope or severity of the crisis.

Constituent correspondence, including letters, emails, or faxes, describing personal experiences with insurance problems is responsive to this issue. Such correspondence is responsive if it describes premium increases, cancellations, inability to obtain coverage, or financial hardship, even if the document does not contain policy analysis or regulatory discussion.

Issue 2: Hurricane Losses and Catastrophic Events
A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to insured losses caused by the 2004 or 2005 Florida hurricane seasons, either individually or in aggregate. Relevant hurricanes include Charley, Frances, Ivan, Jeanne, and Wilma.

A document is responsive to this issue if it reflects information received by the Governor's Office about hurricane damage assessments, insurance claims data, or loss projections.

A document is also responsive if it relates to emergency measures taken in response to hurricane damage and connects those measures to the insurance market, insurance claims, insurance coverage, insurer solvency, market stability, or related insurance consequences.

Issue 3: Regulatory Actions and Omissions
A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to rate increase filings submitted to OIR during the crisis period or to actions taken by OIR on those filings. This includes documents concerning approval, disapproval, actuarial review, rate adequacy, rate excessiveness, or the timing and handling of rate filings.

A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to emergency orders, market conduct examinations, enforcement actions, or other regulatory actions undertaken by OIR in connection with the insurance crisis.

A document is responsive to this issue if it reflects communications between the Governor's Office and OIR regarding the insurance crisis, rate approvals, market stability, insurer conduct, cancellations, non-renewals, or related insurance regulatory matters. Such communications are responsive even if they do not contain substantive policy analysis. Scheduling emails, meeting requests, or transmittal messages are responsive if the document's text indicates that a meeting or communication about insurance regulatory matters occurred.

A document is also responsive if it relates to actions by state regulators to prevent or limit mass policy cancellations, non-renewals, market exits, or related withdrawal activity by private insurers.

Issue 4: Executive Action and Inaction
A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to steps taken or considered by the Governor's Office to address the Florida insurance crisis, including when those steps were taken or considered.

A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to reform proposals, task forces, committees, study groups, or legislative initiatives that the Governor's Office initiated, supported, opposed, considered, or influenced in connection with the insurance crisis.

A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to the Governor's Office's awareness of, or response to, the crisis. This includes information available to the Governor's Office after the 2004 hurricane season regarding the likelihood of continuing market disruption and documents showing what action or inaction followed.

A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to interactions between the Governor's Office and the insurance industry, including private carriers, industry associations, agents, brokers, reinsurers, or lobbyists, concerning reform proposals, regulatory action, market stability, rate increases, cancellations, non-renewals, or other insurance-crisis issues.

Task force, committee, and study group documents are responsive to this issue when they relate to insurance reform during the crisis period. Responsive bodies include the Property and Casualty Insurance Reform Committee chaired by Lt. Governor Toni Jennings, the Coastal High Hazard Study Committee, the Task Force on Long-Term Hurricane Solution, and any other formally constituted body addressing insurance reform during the crisis period. Documents concerning the Property Tax Reform Committee are responsive only if they explicitly address the intersection of insurance costs and property tax burden.

Issue 5: Citizens Operations and Governance
A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to the rate of policy growth at Citizens during the crisis period or the factors that drove that growth, including insurer exits, cancellations, non-renewals, or displacement of policyholders into Citizens.

A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to financial deficits accumulated by Citizens or assessments levied, planned, anticipated, projected, or discussed against Florida policyholders.

A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to governance issues at Citizens, including conflicts of interest, administrative costs, commission structures, senior management issues, board issues, or management of outside counsel.

A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to claims-processing delays, claims-handling failures, or other operational failures at Citizens, including the impact of those delays or failures on policyholders.

A document is responsive to this issue if it reflects communications between the Governor's Office and Citizens concerning Citizens' growth, finances, assessments, governance, rates, depopulation, claims handling, or other Citizens operations.

Documents concerning Citizens' depopulation programs are responsive. Documents concerning rate-setting disputes within Citizens, including competing rate increase proposals for specific counties or regions, are also responsive.

Issue 6: Legislative and Policy Reforms
A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to legislative bills addressing property insurance that were introduced, debated, passed, defeated, proposed, amended, or considered during the crisis period.

A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to alternative solutions to the property insurance crisis. Such solutions include securitization mechanisms, catastrophe bonds, regional risk-sharing pools, sales-tax-funded insurance models, state catastrophe funding approaches, and national catastrophe insurance programs.

A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to the role of the Governor's Office in shaping, supporting, opposing, evaluating, or influencing specific property insurance reform proposals.

A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to mitigation programs that were proposed, funded, implemented, or evaluated, or to evidence about whether mitigation programs could reduce insurance costs.

Documents discussing building code standards, wind mitigation requirements, or home hardening programs are responsive only if the document connects those topics to insurance reform, insurance cost reduction, insurance availability, or the property insurance crisis. Documents addressing building codes in isolation, without any insurance nexus, are not responsive to this issue.


Issue 7: Related Insurance Lines
A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to market instability, reform proposals, or regulatory action concerning workers' compensation insurance during the crisis period, including the operations of the Florida Workers' Compensation JUA.

A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to market instability, reform proposals, or regulatory action concerning medical malpractice insurance during the crisis period, including reform measures proposed or enacted.

A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to connections between the property insurance crisis and instability in other insurance lines, including proposals to address multiple insurance lines through coordinated reform.

Documents concerning workers' compensation, medical malpractice, health insurance, or title insurance are responsive only if they relate to market instability, reform proposals, or regulatory action during the crisis period. Routine administration of these insurance lines without a connection to market disruption or reform is not responsive.

Documents referencing the Florida Health Insurance Plan, the Affordable Health Care for Floridians Act, or small group health insurance reform are responsive if they reflect broader insurance market instability or coordinated reform efforts. Documents concerning only routine health insurance administration are not responsive to this issue.

Issue 8: Economic Consequences
A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to the economic impact of the Florida insurance crisis on Florida's real estate market, commercial sector, housing market, or overall economy.

A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to the fiscal impact of the insurance crisis on the State of Florida. Responsive subject matter includes mitigation program costs, Citizens' financial deficits and assessments, FHCF solvency concerns, FHCF bonding capacity, and state fiscal exposure arising from the insurance crisis.

A document is responsive to this issue if it relates to state budget decisions made in response to the insurance crisis, including appropriations for mitigation funding, surplus allocation, emergency expenditures, budget proposals, or appropriation bills addressing insurance.

Examples of responsive subject matter include real estate transaction failures attributed to insurance unavailability, business closures or relocations citing insurance costs, economic analyses of the crisis's impact on Florida, budget proposals addressing insurance, surplus allocation discussions addressing insurance, FHCF solvency analyses, FHCF bonding capacity projections, and assessments levied or projected against Florida policyholders.

General Scope Rules
Temporal Scope
The primary responsive period is January 1, 2000 through December 31, 2006, except where a specific Issue defines a different period or requires a more specific temporal connection.

Documents from 2000–2003 are responsive if they provide context for the crisis, including early warnings about market vulnerability, prior reform efforts, or related insurance market instability such as the 2003 medical malpractice crisis. Documents from January 1, 2004 through December 31, 2006 require a lower threshold for responsiveness when they relate to any Issue.

If the document's text makes its date apparent, the following periods are especially significant for interpreting responsiveness: August–December 2004, the immediate aftermath of the four-hurricane season; October–December 2005, the period of Hurricane Wilma and cumulative market stress; January–July 2006, the peak crisis period before appointment of the Reform Committee; and July–December 2006, the Reform Committee and legislative proposal period.

Geographic Scope
The primary geographic focus is Florida, except where a specific Issue defines a different or broader geographic connection. Documents about national catastrophe insurance, federal insurance programs, global reinsurance, or out-of-state actors are responsive only if the document connects those subjects to Florida's insurance crisis, Florida policyholders, Florida insurance regulation, Florida reform proposals, or Florida fiscal or market impacts.

Insurance Subject-Matter Scope
The primary subject-matter focus is Florida residential property insurance, homeowners insurance, property insurance market disruption, and related Florida insurance reform, except where a specific Issue defines a different subject-matter connection.

Documents concerning other insurance lines are responsive only if they satisfy the criteria stated in the relevant Issue, including the related-insurance-line criteria for workers' compensation, medical malpractice, health insurance, title insurance, or coordinated insurance reform.

Key People and Entities
Documents may be responsive when they involve or mention key actors in connection with any Issue. Key actors include the Governor's Office, Governor Jeb Bush, Lt. Governor Toni Jennings, executive staff and policy advisors, OIR, Insurance Commissioner Kevin McCarty, DFS, CFO Tom Gallagher, Citizens board members and senior management, Citizens outside counsel, Citizens claims adjusters, Citizens depopulation program administrators, FHCF administrators and advisors, state legislators involved in insurance legislation, the Attorney General's Office, FEMA, HUD, Congressional representatives involved in national catastrophe insurance proposals, private carriers, reinsurers, insurance agents and brokers, industry associations, consumer advocates, real estate professionals, community and condominium associations, business associations, chambers of commerce, healthcare providers raising insurance-cost concerns, and Florida constituents communicating about insurance problems.
The presence of a key person or entity alone is not sufficient for responsiveness. The document must connect that person or entity to one or more Issues, except where a specific Issue treats a type of communication as responsive based on its subject matter.
Brief Mentions and Communications
A brief mention is sufficient for responsiveness if the mention relates to any Issue. Communications about the subject matter of any Issue are responsive even if they are short, administrative, or logistical, provided the document's text shows the communication concerns an Issue.
Responsiveness Rules
Issue-Level Aggregation
A document is responsive if any part of it is responsive to any one Issue.
Document Independence
The responsiveness of a document depends solely on the text contained in that document. The reviewer cannot see attachments, parent emails, family members, or other related documents.
No Issue Tagging or Separate Categorization
The system makes a single binary responsive or non-responsive decision for each document. A document should be treated as responsive if it meets the criteria for any Issue, even if it also relates to additional Issues.
Non-Responsive Documents
A document is not responsive if it has no connection to any Issue. A document is not responsive if it concerns insurance topics entirely unrelated to property insurance, casualty insurance, or the related insurance lines specified in the Issues. A document is not responsive if it addresses policy areas with no insurance dimension and no connection to the Florida property insurance crisis, related reform efforts, or related economic or fiscal impacts.
